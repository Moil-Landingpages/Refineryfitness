Implementation brief

The foundations hold — the gap is architecture and proof

Refinery Fitness arrived in the best technical shape of the three clients: a correct canonical, working robots and sitemap files, and a truthful linked entity graph with a real service-area model. What it does not have is more than one page to rank, a way to be contacted other than email, or anything holding those signals in place as the site grows.

Executive decision

Stop protecting the foundations by hand; build the pages the market searches for

Unusual position: There is no critical defect to recover from. The canonical resolves, the schema is truthful, areaServed already names Buda, Kyle, and Hays County, and no unsupported rating is published anywhere. That is rarer than it sounds.

The constraint is architecture. Four genuinely different offers — Strong Start, 1:1 training, mobile training, and virtual coaching — live as sections of a single URL, so “mobile personal trainer Buda” and “online fitness coach Texas” compete for the same page. The second constraint is contact: a prospect who wants to book has an email link and nothing else. Neither is fixed by more homepage copy.

What was deliberately not done: No city pages were created, and San Marcos was not added to the published service area. It sits inside the Hays County the site already claims, which makes it a reasonable inference — but inference is exactly what erodes local entity trust. It is wired into the market model as one boolean, waiting on Jeff's confirmation.

Geographic target model

Where this business actually competes

Codified in lib/market.ts, which the entity graph, the footer strip, and the keyword set all read. Refinery is a service-area business: sessions run in person, at the client's home or garage gym, or over video, so the model publishes a locality with no street address — which is correct, and which also means it is not eligible for a storefront Google Business Profile without a staffed customer-facing location.

Delivery roadmap

Implementation phases

Phase 0Owner · 1–2 days

Supply the facts the site is missing

  1. Provide a public telephone number, or confirm that email-only contact is a deliberate policy.
  2. Confirm whether San Marcos, Dripping Springs, Wimberley, or South Austin are genuinely served by mobile training.
  3. Decide the Google Business Profile question: is there an eligible business address for a service-area profile with a hidden address?
  4. Send the LinkedIn URL noted as outstanding in lib/site.ts, plus any other official profile.
  5. Confirm which client outcomes in the Proof section have written permission, and what evidence supports them.
  6. Confirm the kinesiology training and any certifications, with verification links where they exist.
Phase 1Engineering · SHIPPED

Hold the foundations in place

  1. Codify the market model in lib/market.ts as the single source of truth for place names. Done
  2. Read areaServed, the business description, the footer strip, and the keyword set from it. Done
  3. Add app/manifest.ts, whose declared icons resolve to real assets. Done
  4. Add npm run check:seo, covering status, metadata, canonical, landmark structure, graph integrity, and the discovery files. Done
Phase 2Engineering + owner · 2–4 days

Make the business contactable and accountable

  1. Add a /contact page with the confirmed phone number, email, service area, and one booking path — and add telephone to the business node.
  2. Add /about-jeff-mensing as a real practitioner page: training, credentials with verification links, scope, and approach. Point the Person node's url at it.
  3. Add /privacy and /terms, and link them globally — the site collects enquiries and check-in submissions today with neither.
  4. Complete sameAs once the profile URLs arrive.
  5. Replace every mailto: CTA with the booking or contact flow, and track the click.
Phase 3Content + engineering · 1–2 weeks

Give each program its own destination

  1. Create canonical program pages: /programs/strong-start, /programs/personal-training, /programs/mobile-training, /programs/virtual-coaching.
  2. Each answers the primary intent in its first paragraph, names who it is and is not for, explains the RPMS method as it applies there, states how pricing and scheduling work, renders the coverage statement, and offers one CTA.
  3. The mobile-training page is the strongest local asset in the set — it is the one offer whose value is inherently geographic. Give it the real logistics: travel radius, what equipment Jeff brings, what a garage or home session needs.
  4. Add each page to app/sitemap.ts and build hub-and-spoke internal links with descriptive anchors.
  5. Do not create Buda and Kyle pages by swapping a city name. The mobile-training page already carries the geographic intent honestly.
Phase 4Owner + marketing · 2–6 weeks

Local profiles and genuine authority

  1. If eligible, create the service-area Google Business Profile with the approved name, category, hidden address, service area covering the confirmed cities, hours, and service list.
  2. Keep one approved name, phone, email, and service-area record across Google, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Facebook, and Instagram.
  3. Build Central Texas authority the way this business actually earns it: churches, schools, local sports programmes, and Hays County community organisations.
  4. Create a permission-based review process. Never gate reviews or mark testimonials up as an aggregate rating.
Phase 5Engineering · ongoing

Verify, measure, iterate

  1. Run npm run check:seo -- https://refineryfitness.biz after deploy and wire it into CI.
  2. Confirm the www variant redirects to the apex in one hop.
  3. Submit the sitemap in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools once the program pages are live.
  4. Consider naming OAI-SearchBot explicitly in robots.ts; the current blanket allow covers it, but naming it documents the intent. Treat GPTBot as a separate decision.
  5. Measure hero and LCP media with field Core Web Vitals — the page is image-heavy and animation-heavy.
  6. Record the search, AI-referral, and enquiry baseline before the traffic from these changes arrives.

Release gate

Definition of done

Ticked items are verified in this release. Circled items are gated on owner-supplied facts and remain open.

  • The route returns 200 with a unique title, description, and one correct self-canonical.
  • /robots.txt, /sitemap.xml, and the manifest return 200.
  • The sitemap contains only canonical, indexable, successful URLs.
  • Structured data parses, every @id reference resolves, and the graph matches visible facts.
  • No unsupported rating, address, or credential is published.
  • One coverage statement drives copy, schema, and keywords from a single file.
  • One <header> and one <main> landmark, and exactly one <h1>.
  • Representative routes pass an automated rendered-metadata smoke test.
  • A telephone number is published and present in the business node.
  • Contact, About, Privacy, and Terms pages exist and are linked globally.
  • Each program with distinct search intent has its own canonical page.
  • sameAs lists every official profile, LinkedIn included.
  • The Google Business Profile eligibility decision is made and acted on.
  • Client outcomes have documented permission and reviewed evidence.
  • The www variant is confirmed to redirect once on production.
  • A search, AI-referral, and enquiry baseline is recorded.
